My dear Cutie,

I have started this letter so many times. I wrote a line, crossed it out, and wrote it again. Because how do you put into words something that has lived quietly in your heart for so long? So tonight I stopped trying to make it perfect. I am simply going to write it the way I feel it — honestly, and from the deepest part of my heart.

Do you remember the first time I saw you? You probably don't. But I do. I remember it more clearly than most days of my life. I was so happy that day — the kind of happy that doesn't need a reason. And right in the middle of that happiness, a small, silly fear crept in: what if she isn't Gujarati? I smile at it now, but that day it truly worried me.

A few days later I found out that you are Gujarati — and inside, I was like, "Oh my God!" And when I came to know that we are both from the same city, I was honestly shocked. Out of all the people in this world, out of all the places we could have come from… the same city. I don't know if it meant anything. But to me, it felt like a small gift that life had quietly placed in my hands.

Can I tell you a little secret? Please don't be angry… you walk like an aunty. And your voice — it's a little like a kau-kau lady. But do you know what? I love it. I love every little thing about you, exactly the way it is. The things I tease you about are the very things I would miss the most.

And your hair… so beautiful, and so long. Some days I would just notice it and forget what I was about to say.

I tried so many times to win your heart, and I couldn't. Maybe in another life I will find you again, and try once more — and I hope that time, my side will be a little heavier.

But do you know what I admire about you the most? It is not only that you are beautiful. It is that you are so much more mature than me. I know I am a little childish. Life feels too heavy for me sometimes; there are days I can barely manage anything at all. And then I look at you — so young, carrying so much, and still meeting your work with a positive heart, every single day. I always wondered, how does she do it? You are brave. You are courageous. And I have never, not even once, seen you cry.

One day I had my own experience with some residents, and it hurt me. I went home feeling really bad. And that was the day I truly understood how hard it must be for you — because you go through it every day, and still you smile. That day, what I felt for you became something much deeper than just liking you. It became respect.

When some people found out that I like you, they started giving their opinions. They tried to change the way I see you; they wanted me to judge you. But I never did. Not once. Because what I saw in you was real, and no one's words could ever change that.

I want you to know this, truly — I have never put this much effort into anyone. Never. I did it only because it was you. Because I really, truly like you.

And still, I could never say any of this to your face. Every time I tried, the words got lost somewhere between my heart and my lips. So I am writing them here, where they can finally reach you.

But I understand. Maybe you don't feel the same. Maybe I should have simply asked you. Maybe you don't like me, or maybe there is a reason I will never know — and you don't owe me any explanation. Whatever it is, I respect it, completely. Your happiness matters more to me than my own wish.

I truly hope you become successful and achieve every single thing you have ever dreamed of. You deserve all of it — and so much more.

I accept my defeat — quietly, and with a full heart. And I am sorry. If I ever caused you any trouble, or hurt you in any way, even without knowing it… please forgive me. I won't trouble you anymore. I promise.

Please take care of yourself. Rest when you are tired, and don't carry everything alone. Keep smiling the way you do — the world is a little brighter because of it.

Once again, I am sorry.
With all my respect, and a piece of my heart
that will always wish you well,
Yours, always a well-wisher
